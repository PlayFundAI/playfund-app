Why this screen looks like this

Each decision traces to the survey, an operator interview, or the teardown.

  • The true-cost receipt leads

    Hidden and unpredictable total cost was the second-biggest theme in 514 free-text answers, and travel was the single most-named cost at 42%. We show travel as an estimate we don't charge, so the number can't ambush them later.

    Survey · 639 parents

  • No account, no app

    App fatigue dominated the free text. Roughly a quarter of shoppers abandon at forced registration.

    TeamSnapRequires an account, and registrations vanish from the dashboard after one year — their help doc advises printing receipts first.

  • Both kids, one plan

    66% of respondents have two or more kids playing. This is a wedge, not an edge case.

    TeamSnapInstallment plans cap at $10,000 per registration and switch off entirely below $5 per payment — so multi-kid families lose the plan.

  • The split sits at the price

    High-converting checkouts show "4 payments of $X" under the total rather than hiding it behind a payment-method choice. Our current test site does this backwards.

    2026 checkout benchmarks

  • "No added cost to you" is stated, not implied

    47% of parents flinch at fees and interest. Bilt makes fee-free housing rewards an absolute, repeated promise. If the club carries the fee, the parent should hear it in plain words.

    Survey · Bilt teardown

  • Invite another payer

    Flywire's authorized-user feature helped institutions cut payment-plan defaults from 34% to under 1%. In youth sports that's a grandparent or a second household. Nobody in the category offers it.

    Flywire

  • A decline is a fork, not a wall

    Klarna carries the credit in the pilot, so some families will be declined — and they're the ones who needed flexibility most. Three real options, and the club sees they tried.

    Pilot structure · locked decision

  • Automatic reminders, always

    A text two days before each charge is how you prevent a failed payment. Every prevented failure is a default that never happens.

    TeamSnap"Participants will not be automatically notified of changes. You can email participants to let them know."

Why this screen looks like this

The club view has one job: replace uncertainty with a date and a number.

  • The payout and the date are the hero

    Travis called the upfront payout "fantastic" and asked to be contacted first. Square charges 1.75% for instant transfer and merchants pay it — speed of access to your own money is a product people buy.

    Travis · 300 Spartans · Square teardown

  • One number, no comparison math

    The fee conversation belongs in the sales call, not the dashboard. Once a club is live, the only thing this screen owes them is what lands and when. Cost comparisons here just invite second-guessing a decision they already made.

    Design call · simplified from v1

  • Declines are on the front page

    Because Klarna carries the credit in the pilot, "paid day one" is really "paid day one for every approved athlete." A club that discovers that gap in March is a club we lose. So it's the third column, visible from day one.

    Pilot structure · locked decision

  • Status written in club language

    "Link opened, not finished." "Declined, chose deposit." Names things by what the operator recognizes, not by how the system works.

    TeamSnap"In Progress - Checkout" and "Pay Offline - Waiting for Payment," plus registrant vs participant vs contact 1, 2, 3.

  • Nobody has to make the awkward call

    Skip Gilbert named the real pain: collections are socially awkward because administrators personally know the families. The system has already offered every declined family an alternative before the club sees the list.

    Skip Gilbert · former US Youth Soccer CEO

  • Built for a volunteer, not an admin

    One screen answers the only two questions a treasurer has: how much, and when. Toast onboarding runs four to six weeks; Square takes minutes. Our buyer is a volunteer with high turnover.

    Toast vs Square teardown

  • Sibling plans are visible

    "Same plan as sibling" tells the operator that multi-kid families are handled — the exact case where incumbent installment plans break.

    Survey · 66% have 2+ kids

Why this screen looks like this

Greg Kristof named adoption as the biggest risk. This flow is the answer to it.

  • Value before friction

    The payout number appears at step 2, before we ask for an EIN or a bank account. A treasurer should know what this is worth before doing paperwork.

    Square's order, not Toast's

  • Eight minutes, not four weeks

    Our buyer is a volunteer with high turnover. One sitting, or never.

    Toast"A typical remote or onsite implementation takes four to six weeks."

  • Paste, never retype

    The roster step is the highest-probability abandonment point in the product. Any column order, forgiving parser, and errors that don't block you from continuing.

    Flagged in flows v2 as top drop-off risk

  • The bank step is resumable by design

    Most volunteer treasurers won't have the EIN handy. Rather than lose them, we save state and text a link back. KYC is the known cliff — the fix is letting people leave and return.

    Stripe Connect · onboarding teardown

  • No demo call, no contract, public pricing

    LeagueApps, PlayMetrics, SportsEngine and Stack Sports all gate pricing behind "request a demo," and several charge a setup fee. Self-serve signup with a visible rate is itself the differentiator with small clubs.

    Competitor pricing teardown

  • The approval caveat sits before they send

    The last thing a club reads before sharing the link is that some families will need a second option. Saying it here costs nothing. Letting them find out in March costs us the reference customer.

    Pilot structure · locked decision
